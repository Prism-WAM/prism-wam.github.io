TL;DR

Plan the goal in Semantic Space (RGB), roll out the future in Physical Space (Depth), and read Actions off the physical rollout.

Abstract

World Action Models (WAMs) typically represent the world through latent embeddings from pretrained video models, which entangle heterogeneous aspects of the world, from how it looks (e.g., texture and color) to how it is physically configured (e.g., occupancy and pose). This raises an underexplored question: what should constitute the state of a WAM? We argue that the world state should separate how the world appears from how the world evolves under action: the former captures semantic context, while the latter is governed by physical state.

To this end, we propose PrismWAM, which decouples physics from semantics while jointly modeling state and action. PrismWAM factorizes an embodied task into a Semantic Planning → Physical Rollout → Executable Action pipeline: given only the initial RGB observation, semantic planning learns to predict the final goal image, while physical rollout predicts the fine-grained evolution toward this goal in depth space; executable actions are then generated from the depth rollout. Without any embodied action pretraining or additional data augmentation, PrismWAM achieves strong manipulation performance in both simulation and the real world. Notably, PrismWAM even surpasses a broad range of models with large-scale embodied action pretraining (e.g., OpenWAM-α). More broadly, we reframe World Action Models from modeling how the world looks to how it responds to action, paving the way for action-centric world models grounded in occupancy, flow, tactile sensing, proprioception, and beyond.

    Key Insight

    Appearance tells you what to act on. Physics tells you what actions change.

    World action models jointly predict video and actions, but a video latent mixes how things look with how they are arranged. PrismWAM splits the state like light through a prism: semantic context stays in RGB, physical state is modelled in depth.

    How It Works

    PrismWAM decouples semantic planning from physical rollout

    We instantiate this principle with a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture built upon a pretrained video backbone. During training, the semantic planner predicts the target from the initial RGB observation and transfers its task-level knowledge to the physical rollout via attention layers, while the action branch learns to map the predicted physical evolution to continuous actions.

    Robustness

    Robust to sensor-like depth noise

    Real depth sensors can add measurement noise and miss readings. We corrupt simulated depth at three sensor-like levels, guided by manufacturers' reported error characteristics, and train and evaluate a model at each. At Level 3, the strongest, PrismWAM reaches 68.30% average success on RoboTwin clean → random, 2.45 points below clean depth.
